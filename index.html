The model is deliberately conservative. Cameras doing continuous tracking are charged for the sampled look as well, even though on the proof of concept they produced their heatmap from the tracking pass at no extra cost. Sizing against the higher figure leaves that saving as margin.

Counting and direction of travel cost the same. Both run continuous tracking at 640 pixels through the same path. Direction of travel does slightly less work, so swapping one for the other is free and its cost is measured rather than estimated.

Tracking is per camera. A person keeps an identity inside one camera's view and the track ends when they leave frame; no identity is carried between cameras. Movement across the mall is reported at the aggregate level, and camera views can be laid out together on the dashboard so the flow between them reads as one picture.

What this is based on, and what it is not▾

Measured, not claimed. The proof of concept ran continuously for three weeks on SM's own test machine, carrying eight cameras: three entrance counts and five per-person dwell, each also producing a heatmap from the same pass. Every figure here comes from timings on that machine.

A planning estimate, not a quotation. It is not a performance guarantee either. Sizing is done per property — an average across an estate sizes nothing, because a flagship mall can carry double the cameras of a typical one.

Sized for production. These figures describe the production platform, including the stream-handling layer built during implementation. The proof of concept was run to measure processing cost per camera, not at production camera density.
